1. WHAT IS THE TEACHER ROLE?
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

As a teacher you are responsible for:

  • Adding and managing your own students
  • Building a library of questions (the Question Bank)
  • Creating exams, setting the time limit, passing grade, and access tokens
  • Distributing those access tokens to students so they can enter the exam
  • Reviewing results after students finish — auto-graded instantly, essays later
  • Exporting class progress reports to Excel at the end of term

What a teacher CANNOT do:
  • See or edit other teachers' exams or questions
  • Access the Admin panel
  • Change their own capabilities (only the admin can grant extra powers)
  • Grant other teachers access

NOTE: Some features you see in the sidebar may be locked with "(locked by admin)"
labels. This means the admin has not yet enabled that feature for your account.
Contact your administrator if you need access to something you cannot use.

2. HOW TO LOG IN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. Open your browser and go to the school's exam website address.
  2. You will see a white card in the centre of the screen:
       • "Exam Dashboard" logo at the top
       • A USERNAME field
       • A PASSWORD field
       • A green "Sign in" button
  3. Type your username (given to you by the administrator).
  4. Type your password.
  5. Click the green "Sign in" button.
  6. You land on your Overview page.

If you cannot log in, ask your administrator to reset your password.
To sign out at any time, look at the bottom-left corner of the screen —
you will see your name and a "Sign out" button.

3. UNDERSTANDING YOUR DASHBOARD LAYOUT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The screen has two parts:

LEFT SIDEBAR (always visible):
  Shows "Exam Dashboard" at the top, then "Teacher" below it.
  Below that are four groups of navigation links:

  WORKSPACE
    └─ Overview         → your home screen with summary numbers

  CLASS
    └─ Students         → add and manage your students

  CONTENT
    ├─ Curriculum       → upload learning objectives (LOs)
    ├─ AI Generation    → use AI to generate question prompts
    ├─ Question Bank    → browse, create, and manage questions
    └─ Exams            → create and manage your exams

  RESULTS
    ├─ Auto Score       → see auto-graded student results
    ├─ Pending Score    → manually grade essay questions
    ├─ Scores           → combined scores view
    └─ Reports          → export class achievement to Excel

  At the very bottom:
    Your full name, your username, and a "Sign out" button.

MAIN AREA (right side):
  Changes depending on which link you click in the sidebar.

4. OVERVIEW — YOUR HOME SCREEN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

When you first log in, you land on the Overview page.
Heading: "Overview" — "Welcome back, [Your Name]."

WHAT YOU SEE:
  Five summary boxes at the top showing your current totals:
    • Exams             — how many exams you have created
    • Total submissions — how many times students have completed your exams
    • Passed            — how many submissions scored above the passing grade
    • Awaiting grading  — how many essay answers still need your manual score
    • Students          — total students in your roster

Below that:
  "Recent submissions" panel
    Shows the last 10 student completions across all your exams.
    Each row shows the student name, exam, score, and a "View" link
    that opens that student's full answer sheet.
    If no one has submitted yet, it shows "No submissions yet."

Nothing on this page is clickable except the "View" links in the submissions
table. It is a read-only summary.

5. STUDENTS — MANAGING YOUR CLASS ROSTER
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Click "Students" in the left sidebar.

Page heading: "Students — X students across Y groups"
Below the heading: "Upload an Excel/CSV file (one sheet per class) to add a
whole class at once."

BUTTONS AT THE TOP:
  ┌─────────────────┐  ┌──────────┐  ┌─────────────┐
  │ Upload Excel    │  │ Bulk add │  │ Add student │
  └─────────────────┘  └──────────┘  └─────────────┘

─────────────────────────────────────────
5a. ADD A SINGLE STUDENT
─────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Click "Add student".
  2. An inline form appears directly on the page with three fields:
       • USERNAME  — 3 to 32 characters. Only letters, digits, dots, dashes,
                     and underscores. The system automatically makes it
                     UPPERCASE. Example: ahmad.fauzi becomes AHMAD.FAUZI
       • FULL NAME — the student's display name
       • PASSWORD  — the password the student will use to log in
  3. Fill in all three fields.
  4. Click the "Add student" button at the bottom of the form.
  5. A confirmation message appears: "Added [FULL NAME] ([USERNAME])."
  6. The student appears in the list under a group called "No class"
     (until you assign them to a class group via Upload Excel).

The "Add student" button in the header changes to "Close" while the form
is open. Click "Close" to cancel without adding.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5b. BULK ADD MULTIPLE STUDENTS AT ONCE
─────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Click "Bulk add".
  2. A "Bulk add students" panel appears with:
       • ROSTER text area — paste student data here, one student per line.
         Two formats are accepted:
           Simple:   Full Name
                     (system generates username and password automatically)
           Detailed: Full Name, username, password
                     (or tab-separated: Full Name [TAB] username [TAB] password)
         Example:
           Ahmad Fauzi
           Siti Aisyah, siti.aisyah, rahasia123
           Budi Santoso
       • ADD TO CLASS (OPTIONAL) dropdown — choose a class to assign them to.
         Only shows classes that already exist in the system.
       • "Create students" button — click to create all students at once.
  3. The system will create an account for every name you pasted.
     Usernames and passwords are shown in the confirmation.
     WRITE THESE DOWN — students will need them to log in.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5c. UPLOAD A WHOLE CLASS FROM EXCEL
─────────────────────────────────────────
  1. Click "Upload Excel".
  2. A file picker opens. Choose your .xlsx or .csv file.
  3. Each sheet in the workbook becomes one class group.
     Each row = one student (columns: full name, username, password).
  4. The system imports and creates all accounts.
  Best for: first setup at the start of term.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5d. VIEWING YOUR STUDENT LIST
─────────────────────────────────────────
  After adding students, they appear in expandable class groups.

  "Expand all" link — expands every group so you can see all students.
  "Collapse all" link — collapses everything back.

  Each group header shows:
    [Expand/Collapse button]  [Class Name]  [X students]
    "Select all" checkbox (for bulk actions)

  Each student row shows:
    • Checkbox (to select for bulk actions)
    • FULL NAME (bold)
    • username (in monospace font)
    • Password (visible in monospace — you can see it for handout)
    • "Copy password" button (copies to clipboard)
    • Submission count ("X submissions")
    • Three action buttons:
        [Reset password]  [Disable]  [Delete student]

─────────────────────────────────────────
5e. PER-STUDENT ACTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────
  Reset password:
    Opens a small form to set a new password for that student.
    Useful if a student forgets their password.

  Disable:
    Blocks the student from logging in without deleting their records.
    Useful if a student leaves mid-term. Their submission history is kept.
    Click again to re-enable.

  Delete student:
    Permanently removes the student and all their submission data.
    Use with caution — this cannot be undone.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5f. BULK ACTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────
  Tick the "Select all" checkbox at the top of a group, or tick individual
  students. Once at least one is ticked, a bulk-action toolbar appears at
  the bottom of the screen offering:
    • Reset passwords (all selected)
    • Deactivate (all selected)
    • Reactivate (all selected)
    • Delete (all selected)
    • Export CSV (download credentials for selected students)

6. CURRICULUM — SETTING UP LEARNING OBJECTIVES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Click "Curriculum" in the left sidebar.

Page heading: "Curriculum"
Description: "Upload Excel files of learning objectives per curriculum
framework. Use the tabs below to switch between curricula — each tab has
its own upload + catalog. You only see LOs you uploaded yourself."

WHAT ARE LEARNING OBJECTIVES (LOs)?
  LOs are used to link your exam questions to a specific curriculum standard.
  For example: "Students can explain Newton's Second Law" is a learning
  objective for Physics. When you generate questions with AI, you can pick
  specific LOs so the AI writes questions that target those skills.

FOUR TABS at the top of the page:
  • Kurikulum Merdeka   (Indonesian national curriculum)
  • AS / A Level (Cambridge)
  • International Baccalaureate (IB)
  • Olympiad

  Each tab shows how many LOs you have in that framework (the number in the
  tab badge). Click a tab to switch to that framework.

BUTTONS IN THE HEADER:
  "Upload Excel → [Framework Name]"
    Click to upload an Excel file of LOs for the currently selected tab.
    The button label changes to match the active tab, e.g.:
      "Upload Excel → Merdeka" or "Upload Excel → Cambridge"
    Excel format: rows with columns for topic, subtopic, learning objective.
    The label also shows "Goes into [Framework Name] · click another tab
    below to change".

  "Add LO" (at the bottom of each tab's content)
    Click to add a single learning objective manually.
    A small inline form appears with:
      • TOPIC              — the main topic area (e.g., "Kinematics")
      • SUBTOPIC (OPTIONAL) — a sub-area (e.g., "Projectile Motion")
      • LEARNING OBJECTIVE — the exact skill statement
    Click "Save LO" to save, or "Cancel" to close the form.

AFTER ADDING LOs:
  They appear as a searchable, filterable catalog in the tab.
  You can edit or delete any LO you added.
  These LOs will then be available when you use AI Generation to create
  exam questions.

7. AI GENERATION — CREATING EXAM QUESTIONS WITH AI
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Click "AI Generation" in the left sidebar.

Page heading: "Generate exam with AI"
Description: "Two phases. Phase 1: download the question prompt JSON and run
it in ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini to get a questions.json. Phase 2: copy the
image prompt + that questions.json into an AI image generator to produce a
media/ folder. Zip them together and upload via Question Bank → Upload
questions."

IMPORTANT: This is a two-step process. The system does NOT call AI directly
(unless your administrator has set up an API key and granted you server-side
generation). Instead, you download a ready-made prompt, paste it into an AI
chatbot yourself, and then upload the AI's response back into the system.

─────────────────────────────────────────
7a. THE PARAMETERS PANEL (left side)
─────────────────────────────────────────

SCOPE section:
  • CURRICULUM dropdown
      Choose a curriculum framework (Merdeka, Cambridge, IB, Olympiad).
      If you leave it as "— none —", you write your own topic instead.

  • LANGUAGE
      Usually "English". May say "locked by admin" — cannot be changed.

  • SUBJECT
      Your subject as recorded in your account (e.g., "PHYSICS / FISIKA").
      Usually pre-filled and locked.

  • GRADE / LEVEL (optional)
      Type the year level or grade, e.g., "Year 11" or "Grade 10".

  • TOPIC
      The main topic for this exam generation, e.g., "Forces and Motion".

  • SUBTOPIC (optional)
      A more specific area within the topic.

QUESTION MIX section:
  • TOTAL QUESTIONS
      How many questions to generate. Default is 10.

  • DIFFICULTY (%) — Bloom's Taxonomy levels
      Seven sliders, one for each cognitive level. They must add up to 100%.
      The levels are: Remember / Understand / Apply / Analyze /
                      Evaluate / Create / Olympiad
      If they all show "(locked by admin)", the admin controls the mix
      and you cannot change it.

      QUICK PRESET BUTTONS (if unlocked):
        [Intro]    — easier mix, mostly Remember/Understand
        [Moderate] — balanced mix across levels
        [Extreme]  — harder mix, mostly Evaluate/Create/Olympiad

  • TYPE COUNTS — per question type
      Single choice / Multi select / Short text / Numeric / Essay
      If locked, you cannot change these.

  • MEDIA
      Images — number of questions to include an image.
      Tables — number of questions to use a markdown table.
      If locked, leave at 0.

─────────────────────────────────────────
7b. PHASE 1 — GENERATE QUESTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────
  The right side of the screen shows a ready-made prompt.

  You have two options:

  Option A — Download the prompt file:
    Click "Download question prompt (JSON)" to save a .json file.
    Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
    Upload or paste the contents of the .json file and say:
      "Please generate exam questions based on this prompt."
    Save the AI's reply as a file called questions.json.

  Option B — Copy to clipboard:
    Click "Copy" to copy the prompt text.
    Paste it into any AI chatbot and ask it to generate questions.
    Save the response as questions.json.

─────────────────────────────────────────
7c. PHASE 2 — GENERATE IMAGES (optional)
─────────────────────────────────────────
  If your exam includes image questions (MEDIA → Images > 0):
    The Phase 2 section shows an image-generation prompt.
    Copy that prompt and the questions.json from Phase 1.
    Paste both into an AI image generator (like DALL·E or Midjourney).
    The AI creates images named q1.png, q2.png, etc.
    Save them into a folder called "media/".

─────────────────────────────────────────
7d. UPLOADING THE RESULT TO YOUR BANK
─────────────────────────────────────────
  Once you have questions.json (and optionally the media/ folder):
    1. Create a ZIP file containing questions.json and the media/ folder.
    2. Go to Question Bank → click "Upload questions".
    3. Upload the ZIP file.
    4. The system imports all questions into your bank automatically.

  After that, you can add those questions to any exam using
  "Pick from bank" or "Auto-fill from bank" on the exam detail page.

Page heading: "Question Bank — X questions shown"
Description: "Filter by subject, topic, subtopic, difficulty, or type.
Read-only shared bank. Questions you upload show here too, and you can

The question bank is SHARED with other teachers (read-only view of theirs)
but you can only edit and delete the questions you yourself uploaded.

  ┌──────────────────┐  ┌──────────────────────┐

SIX FILTER DROPDOWNS below the heading:
  All languages / All subjects / All topics / All subtopics /

  Plus a search box: "Search prompt / topic…"

EXPANDABLE REFERENCE:
  "Upload format reference (click to expand)" — click to see the expected

─────────────────────────────────────────
8a. CREATE A NEW QUESTION MANUALLY
─────────────────────────────────────────
  Click "New question".
  An inline form appears: "New bank question — Added to the shared admin
  database; you keep edit / delete rights on it."

  FIELD 1 — TYPE (dropdown):
    Choose the question format:
      • Single choice     — one correct answer from a list (classic MCQ)
      • Multi select      — one or more correct answers (students tick all
                            that apply; partial credit applies)
      • Short text        — student types a short word or phrase
      • Numeric           — student types a number; tolerance bands apply
      • Structured / Essay — open-ended; graded manually by you

  FIELD 2 — PROMPT:
    Type the full question text here.
    You can use $...$ for inline maths and $$...$$ for block maths.

  FOR SINGLE CHOICE AND MULTI-SELECT — OPTIONS section:
    Four text boxes labelled A, B, C, D appear.
    Type each answer option in its box.
    Click "Add option" to add more choices (E, F, …).
    For SINGLE choice: click the radio button (●) next to the CORRECT answer.
    For MULTI select: click the radio buttons next to ALL correct answers.
    The small "×" button on the right of each option removes that option.

  NOTE FOR ESSAY:
    No options section appears. The note says: "Essay questions are graded
    manually — no correct-answer key."

  FIELD 3 — EXPLANATION:
    Optionally type feedback that the student sees after submitting
    (in Try Out mode) or that you see when grading.

  FIELD 4 — POINTS:
    How many marks this question is worth. Default is 1.

  FIELD 5 — TOPIC:
    Tag this question with a topic name (e.g., "Kinematics").
    Used by the filters and by the AI auto-fill.

  FIELD 6 — SUBTOPIC:
    Optional more specific topic tag.

  FIELD 7 — DIFFICULTY (dropdown):
    Choose the Bloom's taxonomy cognitive level:
      Remember / Understand / Apply / Analyze / Evaluate / Create / Olympiad

  FIELD 8 — LANGUAGE:
    Default is "English". Change if the question is in another language.

  FIELD 9 — SUBJECT (dropdown):
    Choose the subject this question belongs to. Pre-filled from your account.

    Set a date and time before which students cannot access the exam.

    Set a date and time after which the exam is locked.

11. AUTO SCORE — REVIEWING AUTO-GRADED RESULTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Page heading: "Auto Score"
Description: "Auto-graded portion only (MCQ, multi-select, short-text,
numeric). Click View to see each student's answer next to the key and
feedback explanation."

WHAT IS AUTO-SCORED?
  The system automatically grades these question types the moment a student
  submits:
    • Single choice    — exact match
    • Multi select     — partial credit: (correct selections − wrong selections)
                         divided by total correct options
    • Short text       — exact text match (case-insensitive)
    • Numeric          — within tolerance bands: 1% → full, 5% → 80%,
                         10% → 50% credit

  Essay / Structured questions are NOT auto-graded. They appear in
  "Pending Score" instead.

HOW IT IS ORGANISED:
  Each exam appears as a collapsible button: "[Exam Name] [CODE] · X submissions"
  Click an exam to expand it and see each student's result.
  Each student row shows their name, submission time, and auto-graded score.
  Click "View" to open the detailed answer review screen.

ON THE ANSWER REVIEW SCREEN:
  Every question is shown with:
    • The question text
    • The student's answer (highlighted)
    • The correct answer
    • The explanation (if you wrote one)
    • The points awarded
  You can override the automatic score by clicking "Edit score" on any question.

12b. AI-ASSISTED ESSAY GRADING
─────────────────────────────────────────
  For large classes, you can use an AI chatbot to suggest scores:

  1. On the Pending Score page, find an export button next to the exam.
     Click it to download a bundle of all pending essay answers.
  2. Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
  3. Upload or paste the bundle and the mark scheme.

15. TYPICAL FIRST-WEEK WORKFLOW (STEP BY STEP)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Below is a recommended end-to-end sequence for a teacher who has just
received their account and wants to run their first exam.

DAY 1 — SETUP

  Step 1: Log in
    Open the website and sign in with the credentials given by the admin.

  Step 2: Add your students
    Go to Students → click "Upload Excel" and upload your class spreadsheet.
    OR click "Bulk add" and paste a list of names.
    OR add them one-by-one with "Add student".
    NOTE DOWN: each student's username and password — they need these.

  Step 3: (Optional) Set up learning objectives
    Go to Curriculum → pick your framework tab → click "Add LO" or
    "Upload Excel" to import your topic list.
    Skip this step for now if you just want a quick first exam.

DAY 2 — BUILD QUESTIONS

  Step 4: Add questions to the bank
    Option A — Manual: Go to Question Bank → click "New question" → fill in
    the form for each question → click "Create question".
    Option B — AI: Go to AI Generation → set parameters → download the
    prompt JSON → paste into ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini → save the response
    as questions.json → zip it → upload via Question Bank → Upload questions.

DAY 3 — CREATE THE EXAM

  Step 5: Create the exam
    Go to Exams → click "Create exam" → fill in:
      • Exam code (e.g., PHY-T1)
      • Display name (e.g., "Physics Term 1 Test")
      • Subject, duration (e.g., 60 minutes), passing grade (e.g., 60%)
      • Choose mode: Strict for a real test, Try Out for practice
    Click "Create exam".

  Step 6: Add questions to the exam
    On the Exam Detail page → click "Pick from bank" → find your questions
    → click "Add" for each one you want.
    OR click "Auto-fill from bank" and the system picks questions for you
    based on your composition targets.

  Step 7: Generate an access token
    On the Exam Detail page → Tokens section → set Max uses (e.g., 35 for
    a class of 35) and Expires in (e.g., 7 days) → click "Generate token".
    Write down the token code (e.g., "PHY-T1-A7K2").

  Step 8: Tell students the token
    Write the exam code and token code on the board before the exam starts.
    Students will enter these when they access the exam page.

EXAM DAY

  Step 9: Students take the exam
    Students open the exam website, enter the exam code and their token,
    then log in with their username and password.
    The exam timer starts automatically.
    If internet is lost, their answers are saved locally and re-synced.

  Step 10: Monitor progress (optional)
    On the Exam Detail page → click "Live monitor" to see who is active,
    how far through they are, and how much time they have left.
    Note: This requires the admin to have set up the live monitor route.

AFTER THE EXAM

  Step 11: Review auto-graded results
    Go to Auto Score → click your exam → see each student's score.
    Click "View" on any student for the full answer breakdown.

  Step 12: Grade essays (if any)
    Go to Pending Score → click "View" for each essay → enter score and
    optional feedback → click "Save grade".

  Step 13: Export results for your records
    Go to Reports → tick the columns you want → click "Export all classes".
    Save the Excel file and submit to administration.

16. IMPORTANT RULES AND GOTCHAS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

★ TOKENS ARE REQUIRED — No student can access any exam without a valid
  access token. If a student says they cannot start the exam, check that
  the token you gave them has not expired, has not been deleted, and has
  not reached its maximum use count.

★ STRICT MODE = NO PAUSING — In Strict mode, the exam timer runs even if
  a student closes the browser. If their internet cuts out during a Strict
  exam, time keeps ticking. Use Try Out mode if you expect unreliable
  internet.

★ ANSWERS ARE SAVED AUTOMATICALLY — Every keystroke is saved to the
  student's browser instantly. The system also saves to the server every
  5 seconds. If a student's browser crashes, their answers are preserved
  and restored when they return. They will see a "Resume" button.

★ TIMER RUNS OUT → AUTO-SUBMIT — When time expires in Strict mode, the
  exam auto-submits. If the student's connection is lost at that moment,
  a server-side job finalises their session within 1 minute. No answers
  are ever lost because of a timeout.

★ ESSAY SCORES ARE PROVISIONAL — A student's final result is marked
  "Pending" until you have graded all their essay questions. The auto-
  graded MCQ portion is scored immediately, but the TOTAL result is not
  final until essays are done.

★ USERNAMES ARE UPPERCASE — The system forces usernames to uppercase.
  "ahmad.fauzi" becomes "AHMAD.FAUZI". Tell students to type their
  username exactly as shown on their credential sheet.

★ PASSWORDS ARE VISIBLE TO TEACHERS — You can see every student's
  password on the Students page. Use the "Copy password" button to copy
  it to the clipboard. This is intentional so you can help students who
  forget their password without resetting it.

★ QUESTION BANK IS SHARED — Other teachers can VIEW your questions but
  cannot edit or delete them. You also see their questions (read-only).
  This is to allow school-wide question sharing. Only upload questions
  you are comfortable sharing.

★ LOCKED FEATURES SAY "(locked by admin)" — If a difficulty slider,
  question type count, or other parameter shows "(locked by admin)", only
  the administrator can unlock it for your account. Contact your admin.

★ COMPOSITION TARGETS ARE ADVISORY — Setting composition targets (e.g.,
  "30 single choice questions") does not prevent you from adding a
  different mix. They are just a checklist to help "Auto-fill from bank"
  choose the right questions.

★ DELETING AN EXAM IS PERMANENT — Deleting an exam also deletes all
  student submissions for that exam. This cannot be undone. Archive or
  export results first.

★ CHANGING THE EXAM CODE IS NOT POSSIBLE — Once created, the short exam
  code (e.g., PHY-T1) cannot be changed. Choose it carefully.

★ MULTIPLE TOKENS ARE FINE — You can create more than one token for an
  exam (e.g., one token per class sitting). Each token tracks its own
  usage count.

★ EXAM PASSWORDS vs TOKENS — Students need BOTH their personal username/
  password (to log into the system) AND an access token (to enter a
  specific exam). These are two separate things.
